# Portfolio Rebalancing Recommendation (demonstration sample; all content is fictional)

**Client**: Ms. Evelyn Lin　**Monitoring date**: 2026-07-20　**Basis**: IPS strategic asset allocation (SAA) policy ranges

## 1. Drift Diagnosis

Since the last review, A-shares have rallied sharply (roughly +31% over the period) while the bond market pulled back modestly (about −3%), and portfolio weights have drifted well off target:

- **Domestic Equity (A-shares / CSI 300)**: target 25.0%, current 36.4% — **above the upper band** (policy range 15%–35%), a +11.4 percentage-point deviation and the main source of this drift;
- **Fixed Income**: target 35.0%, current 23.8% — **below the lower band** (policy range 25%–45%), a −11.2 percentage-point deviation;
- International Equity (19.2%), Alternatives — Gold (11.6%) and Cash Equivalents (9.0%) all sit inside their policy ranges and need no action.

The portfolio's actual risk exposure now resembles a "Growth" profile rather than the "Moderate" profile agreed in your IPS — if equities correct, portfolio volatility would breach your −12% maximum acceptable annual loss anchor.

## 2. Rebalancing Recommendation

We recommend rebalancing back to the SAA target weights. Indicative trades (based on current market values):

| Asset class | Action | Adjustment | Note |
|-------------|--------|------------|------|
| Domestic Equity (A-shares / CSI 300) | Sell | −11.4 pp | Realize part of the gains and lock them in |
| Fixed Income | Buy | +11.2 pp | Rebuild the ballast and damp portfolio volatility |
| Alternatives — Gold | Sell | −1.6 pp | Trim back to the 10% target |
| International Equity (Developed Markets) | Buy | +0.8 pp | Fine-tune back to the 20% target |
| Cash Equivalents | Buy | +1.0 pp | Restore the 10% liquidity reserve |

These trades follow the IPS rebalancing discipline exactly: "any asset class breaching its policy range triggers rebalancing." This round is a textbook "sell high, buy low" — trimming what has outperformed and adding to what has lagged; disciplined execution is itself a source of long-run excess return.

## 3. Execution and Timing

- **Single tranche**: the deviation is large (>10pp) and the trigger unambiguous, so we recommend executing in one go rather than letting drift widen during a phased approach; if preferred, split into two tranches no more than two weeks apart.
- **Cost control**: execute primarily through ETF feeder funds and C-share classes with low redemption fees; estimated transaction costs of about 0.1%–0.2% — far below the potential drawdown cost of leaving the drift unattended.
- **Tax and cash flow**: selling equities realizes book gains, so keep the trade records on file; redirect the proceeds into fixed income and money market instruments the same day.

## 4. Risk Notes

Past performance does not guarantee future results. Rebalancing neither assures a profit nor eliminates the opportunity cost if a sold asset keeps rising. Period returns come from a market snapshot; actual execution prices settle at trade-day NAV. This recommendation is a demonstration sample and does not constitute an investment offer; consider your cash-flow situation before acting and consult a licensed advisor where needed.
